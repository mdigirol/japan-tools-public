# Money and shopping

Feeds Currency, Shopping and tax-free, the Tax-free claim log, and the Souvenir tracker.

## Cash and cards

- Japan is still cash-heavy. Many small restaurants, temples and market stalls take cash only. Kyoto is the most cash-dependent of the trip's cities. [N][K]
- Keep about ¥10,000 or more in mixed notes and coins. The ¥500 coin is the biggest coin, worth roughly $3–4 depending on the rate. Nina's "~$5" is dated. [N][V]
- Bring a coin purse. Coins pile up, and shrines and lockers need them. [T]
- Use 7-Eleven ATMs, which reliably take foreign cards. Skip airport exchange kiosks. [N][T]
- When an ATM or card terminal offers to charge in dollars, choose **yen**. The dollar option uses a worse rate. [N][V]
- No tipping, ever. [N][T]

- **税込 / 税抜.** A price tag marked 税抜 adds 10% at the register (8% on most food to take away). The toggle in the converter should show both. [V]

## Tax-free (the rules change Nov 1, 2026)

From **November 1, 2026**, Japan switches to a refund system. [N][JNTO]

- Shops charge the full price, including 10% tax. You get the tax back at **customs on departure, before check-in**.
- The goods must be with you and shown at customs. Items shipped home separately don't count. [N][JNTO]
- Keep **every receipt**. No receipt, no refund. [N]
- The **¥5,000 minimum (before tax) per store per day** stays the same. Bundle small purchases at one store to reach it. [N]
- The split between general goods and consumables ends. Consumables no longer need sealed bags, but they lose eligibility if used in Japan. [JNTO]
- You have 90 days, up from 30, to take the goods out of Japan. [JNTO]
- Show your passport at the shop; it's logged electronically. [JNTO]
- Look for the "Japan. Tax-free Shop" sign. Don Quijote, Uniqlo, GU, LOFT and Bic Camera participate. [N]
- **For the app:** this is why the tax-free claim log exists. On Nov 27 at Haneda it becomes a checklist of items, receipts and the bag each is packed in, with the total due. Budget extra airport time. [N]
- Source to cite in the app: [JNTO: Changes are coming to Japan's tax-free shopping](https://www.japan.travel/en/ca/news/changes-are-coming-to-japans-tax-free-shopping/). Check the Japan Tourism Agency page for the final procedure before the trip. [V]

## Sizes

- Japanese clothing runs small. Size up once from US sizes, for example US M → JP L. [T]
- Shoes are labeled in centimeters. Rough guide: US men's + 18 = cm (US 9 → 27); US women's + 17 = cm (US 7 → 24). Try on to be sure. [V]

## What's worth buying (for the souvenir tracker's suggestions)

From Nina [N]:

- Skincare and sunscreen. Matsumoto Kiyoshi and other drugstores are calmer than Don Quijote.
- Japanese whisky and gin, cheaper than in the US with a wider range.
- Snacks, tea, seasonings and nori. They travel well.
- Uniqlo, GU and Muji basics. FamilyMart socks.
- Same-day glasses at JINS.
- Kitchen goods: ceramics, knives, chopsticks, onigiri molds. Kappabashi or a department store's kitchen floor.

From the Kyoto guide [K]: buy matcha from a specialist (Ippodo in Kyoto, or Uji shops), not tourist stalls. Ask staff which grade suits how you'll use it.
