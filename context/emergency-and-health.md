## When things go wrong

- **Lost item.** Japan returns lost things remarkably often. Ask at the nearest koban (police box) or the station office. Items left on trains go to the line's lost-and-found. [N][V]
- **Hospitals.** Some don't take walk-ins. Look for international or English-speaking clinics, or call the hotlines above. [T]
- **Passport.** Visitors must carry it. You also need it for tax-free shopping. [T][V]

## Earthquakes

- Small tremors are routine.
- In a strong one: drop, take cover under sturdy furniture, protect your head. Don't run outside while it's shaking. [N]
- Phones sound a loud early warning; follow the on-screen instructions. [N]
- Hotels post bilingual evacuation maps. Find your nearest exit (非常口) at check-in. [N][V]
- NERV is a disaster-alert app worth installing. [T]

## Pharmacy

Drugstores (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Sundrug, Welcia) are well stocked and often open late. A pharmacist can recommend something; some medicines are behind the counter. Google Lens reads ingredient labels well. [N]

Common over-the-counter brands, from Nina's list [N]:

| Need | Brands |
| --- | --- |
| Headache, pain | EVE, Bufferin, Loxonin S (stronger NSAID) |
| Cold, flu | Pabron Gold, Contac 600 |
| Fever | Naron Ace |
| Stomach ache, diarrhea | Seirogan |
| Nausea, motion sickness | Anerongo, Travelmin (take 30 minutes before) |
| Sore throat | Ryukakusan, Vicks drops |
| Indigestion | Ohta's Isan |
| Eye strain | Sante FX, Rohto |
| Insect bites | Muhi |
| Blisters | Band-Aid, Compeed |
| Sleep | Drewell (melatonin is not sold in Japan) |

Notes: pseudoephedrine (Sudafed) is restricted, and Lactaid isn't sold, so bring your own. This list is for orientation, not medical advice. [N]
