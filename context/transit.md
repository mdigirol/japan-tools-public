# Transit helper notes

How-to content, not routing. Google Maps handles routes [T].

## IC cards (Suica / PASMO / ICOCA)

- Tap in at the gate and tap out. The fare comes off automatically. [T][K]
- All major IC cards work nationwide. A Tokyo Suica works in Kyoto, Osaka and Nara, and on buses. [N][K]
- iPhone: Suica in Apple Wallet, topped up with a card from the phone. [N][T]
- Top up a physical card at any station machine: look for チャージ (charge). Machines have English. [N][T]
- Pays at konbini, vending machines, some restaurants. [K][T]
- On the Romancecar, the limited-express seat is booked separately. Tap Suica at the gate for the base fare `[I]`.

## Riding

- Google Maps gives the platform and the best car for transfers, and shows local vs rapid. [T]
- Local (各駅停車) stops everywhere. Rapid (快速) skips stops. Check which you need. [T]
- The route map above the doors has small arrows showing which side opens next. Move there early on crowded trains. [T]
- Big stations have 100+ exits. Pick the exit number in Maps before you walk. [T]
- Rush hours: about 7:30–9:30 AM and 5:30–7:30 PM. [N][T]
- Last trains run about midnight. In Google Maps, use "Leave" → "Last" to see the final train. [T]
- Using the restroom outside the gates means paying again to re-enter. [T]
- Etiquette for riding is in `etiquette.md` (Trains).

## Shinkansen

- **Reading the ticket.** 号車 = car, the number plus letter = seat, 番線 = platform. Car numbers are painted on the platform floor where the doors stop. [V]
- **Reserved vs non-reserved.** Reserved guarantees a seat. Non-reserved cars fill fast on Tokyo–Kyoto. [N]
- **Oversized luggage.** Bags over 160 cm total (H+W+D, wheels included) need a seat with oversized-luggage space, at no extra cost. Without one there's a ¥1,000 fee. Over 250 cm, ship it. [N]
- **Missed train.** With an ordinary reserved ticket you can usually ride a later train the same day in a non-reserved car. Some discounted tickets don't allow this. [N][V]
- **Mt. Fuji.** Window seat E (D/E in Green) on the Fuji side. It's visible about 40–45 minutes out of Tokyo; from Odawara, sooner. A clear day is needed. [N][K][V]
- **Ekiben.** Buy before boarding. Eating on the Shinkansen is normal. [N][K]
- **Smart EX** is JR Central's booking app. It links to your IC card so you tap through the gate. [N][T]

## Kyoto buses and subway

- Board at the **rear** door and pay at the **front** as you get off. In the central flat-fare zone the fare is about ¥230. Tap IC on exit. [K][V]
- The bus-only one-day pass the Kyoto guide mentions was discontinued in 2023. Use IC. [V]
- Buses crawl in autumn traffic. Train plus a 10–15 minute walk is often faster. For example, Fushimi Inari is two stops from Kyoto Station on the JR Nara Line. [K]
- The subway has only two lines. Expect to walk more in Kyoto than in Tokyo. [K]

## Taxis

- A red **空車** in the windshield means free. **賃走** means occupied. **迎車** means on the way to a pickup. [N][T][V]
- The doors open and close on their own. [T]
- GO and Uber both hail ordinary metered taxis and add about ¥100. Register before you need them; signup uses SMS. [N][T]
- Late-night surcharge of about 20% applies from 10 PM to 5 AM. [N][V]
- At airports, use the taxi rank. [N]
- Show the hotel card from the Today screen instead of saying the address. [V]

## Luggage forwarding (takkyubin)

- Ship bags hotel to hotel, usually arriving next day. Ask the front desk the night before, or use a konbini counter or the Yamato counter at the airport. About ¥1,500–2,500 per bag between cities. [K][T]
- Coin lockers in stations hold shopping bags for the day. [T]
- Ecbo Cloak books luggage storage in shops. [T]

## Hakone

- Ropeway and cable-car status can change with volcanic gas or wind, so check the morning you go. [V]
- Fares and the Hakone Freepass are in the itinerary, so don't duplicate them. [I]
